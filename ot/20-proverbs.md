## Date of writing: collections c. 950–700 BC; final form c. 700 BC or Persian period

Proverbs is an anthology with its own headings: "The proverbs of Solomon" (1:1; 10:1), "the words of the wise" (22:17; 24:23), proverbs of Solomon "which the men of Hezekiah king of Judah copied out" (25:1), "the words of Agur" (30:1) and "the words of King Lemuel" (31:1). Its collections therefore come from different times. Hezekiah's scribes (c. 715–686 BC) give a fixed point for chapters 25–29. Conservative scholars place the final form not long after that; most critical scholars put the final edition, with chapters 1–9 as its introduction, in the Persian period.

## Proverbs 1

> **Date written:** c. 950 BC (Solomon) or c. 500–300 BC (critical)
>
> "The proverbs of Solomon, the son of David, king of Israel" (1:1) heads the whole book. Chapters 1–9 are a father's instruction to his son and Wisdom's call in the streets (1:20–33). Many critical scholars date this introduction latest of all.
>
> **Basis:**
> - [Solomon](../reference/anchors.md#solomon), c. 970–931 BC. Forty years (1 Kgs 11:42). The temple was begun in his 4th year (967/966 BC, 1 Kgs 6:1) and finished seven years later (6:38).
>
> **Sources:** [Thiele 1983](../reference/sources.md#thiele); [Galil 1996](../reference/sources.md#galil); [Shoshenq I relief](../reference/sources.md#shoshenq-relief); [Cogan; Cogan & Tadmor](../reference/sources.md#cogan-kings).

<sup>1</sup>The proverbs of Solomon, the son of David, king of Israel:

<sup>2</sup>to know wisdom and instruction;  
&emsp;&emsp;to discern the words of understanding;  
<sup>3</sup>to receive instruction in wise dealing,  
&emsp;&emsp;in righteousness, justice, and equity;  
<sup>4</sup>to give prudence to the simple,  
&emsp;&emsp;knowledge and discretion to the young man—  
<sup>5</sup>that the wise man may hear, and increase in learning;  
&emsp;&emsp;that the man of understanding may attain to sound counsel;  
<sup>6</sup>to understand a proverb and parables,  
&emsp;&emsp;the words and riddles of the wise.  
<sup>7</sup>The fear of the LORD[^1-1] is the beginning of knowledge,  
&emsp;&emsp;but the foolish despise wisdom and instruction.  
<sup>8</sup>My son, listen to your father’s instruction,  
&emsp;&emsp;and don’t forsake your mother’s teaching;  
<sup>9</sup>for they will be a garland to grace your head,  
&emsp;&emsp;and chains around your neck.  
<sup>10</sup>My son, if sinners entice you,  
&emsp;&emsp;don’t consent.  
<sup>11</sup>If they say, “Come with us.  
&emsp;&emsp;Let’s lie in wait for blood.  
&emsp;&emsp;Let’s lurk secretly for the innocent without cause.  
<sup>12</sup>Let’s swallow them up alive like Sheol,[^1-2]  
&emsp;&emsp;and whole, like those who go down into the pit.  
<sup>13</sup>We’ll find all valuable wealth.  
&emsp;&emsp;We’ll fill our houses with plunder.  
<sup>14</sup>You shall cast your lot among us.  
&emsp;&emsp;We’ll all have one purse”—  
<sup>15</sup>my son, don’t walk on the path with them.  
&emsp;&emsp;Keep your foot from their path,  
<sup>16</sup>for their feet run to evil.  
&emsp;&emsp;They hurry to shed blood.  
<sup>17</sup>For the net is spread in vain in the sight of any bird;  
<sup>18</sup>but these lay in wait for their own blood.  
&emsp;&emsp;They lurk secretly for their own lives.  
<sup>19</sup>So are the ways of everyone who is greedy for gain.  
&emsp;&emsp;It takes away the life of its owners.  
<sup>20</sup>Wisdom calls aloud in the street.  
&emsp;&emsp;She utters her voice in the public squares.  
<sup>21</sup>She calls at the head of noisy places.  
&emsp;&emsp;At the entrance of the city gates, she utters her words:  
<sup>22</sup>“How long, you simple ones, will you love simplicity?  
&emsp;&emsp;How long will mockers delight themselves in mockery,  
&emsp;&emsp;and fools hate knowledge?  
<sup>23</sup>Turn at my reproof.  
&emsp;&emsp;Behold,[^1-3] I will pour out my spirit on you.  
&emsp;&emsp;I will make known my words to you.  
<sup>24</sup>Because I have called, and you have refused;  
&emsp;&emsp;I have stretched out my hand, and no one has paid attention;  
<sup>25</sup>but you have ignored all my counsel,  
&emsp;&emsp;and wanted none of my reproof;  
<sup>26</sup>I also will laugh at your disaster.  
&emsp;&emsp;I will mock when calamity overtakes you,  
<sup>27</sup>when calamity overtakes you like a storm,  
&emsp;&emsp;when your disaster comes on like a whirlwind,  
&emsp;&emsp;when distress and anguish come on you.  
<sup>28</sup>Then they will call on me, but I will not answer.  
&emsp;&emsp;They will seek me diligently, but they will not find me,  
<sup>29</sup>because they hated knowledge,  
&emsp;&emsp;and didn’t choose the fear of the LORD.  
<sup>30</sup>They wanted none of my counsel.  
&emsp;&emsp;They despised all my reproof.  
<sup>31</sup>Therefore they will eat of the fruit of their own way,  
&emsp;&emsp;and be filled with their own schemes.  
<sup>32</sup>For the backsliding of the simple will kill them.  
&emsp;&emsp;The careless ease of fools will destroy them.  
<sup>33</sup>But whoever listens to me will dwell securely,  
&emsp;&emsp;and will be at ease, without fear of harm.”  

[^1-1]: When rendered in ALL CAPITAL LETTERS, “LORD” or “GOD” is the translation of God’s Proper Name (Hebrew “יהוה”, usually pronounced Yahweh).
[^1-2]: Sheol is the place of the dead.
[^1-3]: “Behold”, from “הִנֵּה”, means look at, take notice, observe, see, or gaze at. It is often used as an interjection.

## Proverbs 2

> **Date written:** c. 950 BC (Solomon) or c. 500–300 BC (critical)
>
> Part of the introductory instruction (chs. 1–9); see chapter 1 for the dating question.
>
> **Basis:**
> - [Solomon](../reference/anchors.md#solomon), c. 970–931 BC. Forty years (1 Kgs 11:42). The temple was begun in his 4th year (967/966 BC, 1 Kgs 6:1) and finished seven years later (6:38).
>
> **Sources:** [Thiele 1983](../reference/sources.md#thiele); [Galil 1996](../reference/sources.md#galil); [Shoshenq I relief](../reference/sources.md#shoshenq-relief); [Cogan; Cogan & Tadmor](../reference/sources.md#cogan-kings).

<sup>1</sup>My son, if you will receive my words,  
&emsp;&emsp;and store up my commandments within you,  
<sup>2</sup>so as to turn your ear to wisdom,  
&emsp;&emsp;and apply your heart to understanding;  
<sup>3</sup>yes, if you call out for discernment,  
&emsp;&emsp;and lift up your voice for understanding;  
<sup>4</sup>if you seek her as silver,  
&emsp;&emsp;and search for her as for hidden treasures;  
<sup>5</sup>then you will understand the fear of the LORD,  
&emsp;&emsp;and find the knowledge of God.[^2-1]  
<sup>6</sup>For the LORD gives wisdom.  
&emsp;&emsp;Out of his mouth comes knowledge and understanding.  
<sup>7</sup>He lays up sound wisdom for the upright.  
&emsp;&emsp;He is a shield to those who walk in integrity,  
<sup>8</sup>that he may guard the paths of justice,  
&emsp;&emsp;and preserve the way of his saints.  
<sup>9</sup>Then you will understand righteousness and justice,  
&emsp;&emsp;equity and every good path.  
<sup>10</sup>For wisdom will enter into your heart.  
&emsp;&emsp;Knowledge will be pleasant to your soul.  
<sup>11</sup>Discretion will watch over you.  
&emsp;&emsp;Understanding will keep you,  
<sup>12</sup>to deliver you from the way of evil,  
&emsp;&emsp;from the men who speak perverse things,  
<sup>13</sup>who forsake the paths of uprightness,  
&emsp;&emsp;to walk in the ways of darkness,  
<sup>14</sup>who rejoice to do evil,  
&emsp;&emsp;and delight in the perverseness of evil,  
<sup>15</sup>who are crooked in their ways,  
&emsp;&emsp;and wayward in their paths,  
<sup>16</sup>to deliver you from the strange woman,  
&emsp;&emsp;even from the foreigner who flatters with her words,  
<sup>17</sup>who forsakes the friend of her youth,  
&emsp;&emsp;and forgets the covenant of her God;  
<sup>18</sup>for her house leads down to death,  
&emsp;&emsp;her paths to the departed spirits.  
<sup>19</sup>None who go to her return again,  
&emsp;&emsp;neither do they attain to the paths of life.  
<sup>20</sup>Therefore walk in the way of good men,  
&emsp;&emsp;and keep the paths of the righteous.  
<sup>21</sup>For the upright will dwell in the land.  
&emsp;&emsp;The perfect will remain in it.  
<sup>22</sup>But the wicked will be cut off from the land.  
&emsp;&emsp;The treacherous will be rooted out of it.  

[^2-1]: The Hebrew word rendered “God” is “אֱלֹהִ֑ים” (Elohim).

## Proverbs 3

> **Date written:** c. 950 BC (Solomon) or c. 500–300 BC (critical)
>
> Part of the introductory instruction (chs. 1–9); see chapter 1 for the dating question.
>
> **Basis:**
> - [Solomon](../reference/anchors.md#solomon), c. 970–931 BC. Forty years (1 Kgs 11:42). The temple was begun in his 4th year (967/966 BC, 1 Kgs 6:1) and finished seven years later (6:38).
>
> **Sources:** [Thiele 1983](../reference/sources.md#thiele); [Galil 1996](../reference/sources.md#galil); [Shoshenq I relief](../reference/sources.md#shoshenq-relief); [Cogan; Cogan & Tadmor](../reference/sources.md#cogan-kings).

<sup>1</sup>My son, don’t forget my teaching,  
&emsp;&emsp;but let your heart keep my commandments,  
<sup>2</sup>for they will add to you length of days,  
&emsp;&emsp;years of life, and peace.  
<sup>3</sup>Don’t let kindness and truth forsake you.  
&emsp;&emsp;Bind them around your neck.  
&emsp;&emsp;Write them on the tablet of your heart.  
<sup>4</sup>So you will find favor,  
&emsp;&emsp;and good understanding in the sight of God and man.  
<sup>5</sup>Trust in the LORD with all your heart,  
&emsp;&emsp;and don’t lean on your own understanding.  
<sup>6</sup>In all your ways acknowledge him,  
&emsp;&emsp;and he will make your paths straight.  
<sup>7</sup>Don’t be wise in your own eyes.  
&emsp;&emsp;Fear the LORD, and depart from evil.  
<sup>8</sup>It will be health to your body,  
&emsp;&emsp;and nourishment to your bones.  
<sup>9</sup>Honor the LORD with your substance,  
&emsp;&emsp;with the first fruits of all your increase;  
<sup>10</sup>so your barns will be filled with plenty,  
&emsp;&emsp;and your vats will overflow with new wine.  
<sup>11</sup>My son, don’t despise the LORD’s discipline,  
&emsp;&emsp;neither be weary of his correction;  
<sup>12</sup>for whom the LORD loves, he corrects,  
&emsp;&emsp;even as a father reproves the son in whom he delights.  
<sup>13</sup>Happy is the man who finds wisdom,  
&emsp;&emsp;the man who gets understanding.  
<sup>14</sup>For her good profit is better than getting silver,  
&emsp;&emsp;and her return is better than fine gold.  
<sup>15</sup>She is more precious than rubies.  
&emsp;&emsp;None of the things you can desire are to be compared to her.  
<sup>16</sup>Length of days is in her right hand.  
&emsp;&emsp;In her left hand are riches and honor.  
<sup>17</sup>Her ways are ways of pleasantness.  
&emsp;&emsp;All her paths are peace.  
<sup>18</sup>She is a tree of life to those who lay hold of her.  
&emsp;&emsp;Happy is everyone who retains her.  
<sup>19</sup>By wisdom the LORD founded the earth.  
&emsp;&emsp;By understanding, he established the heavens.  
<sup>20</sup>By his knowledge, the depths were broken up,  
&emsp;&emsp;and the skies drop down the dew.  
<sup>21</sup>My son, let them not depart from your eyes.  
&emsp;&emsp;Keep sound wisdom and discretion,  
<sup>22</sup>so they will be life to your soul,  
&emsp;&emsp;and grace for your neck.  
<sup>23</sup>Then you shall walk in your way securely.  
&emsp;&emsp;Your foot won’t stumble.  
<sup>24</sup>When you lie down, you will not be afraid.  
&emsp;&emsp;Yes, you will lie down, and your sleep will be sweet.  
<sup>25</sup>Don’t be afraid of sudden fear,  
&emsp;&emsp;neither of the desolation of the wicked, when it comes;  
<sup>26</sup>for the LORD will be your confidence,  
&emsp;&emsp;and will keep your foot from being taken.  
<sup>27</sup>Don’t withhold good from those to whom it is due,  
&emsp;&emsp;when it is in the power of your hand to do it.  
<sup>28</sup>Don’t say to your neighbor, “Go, and come again;  
&emsp;&emsp;tomorrow I will give it to you,”  
&emsp;&emsp;when you have it by you.  
<sup>29</sup>Don’t devise evil against your neighbor,  
&emsp;&emsp;since he dwells securely by you.  
<sup>30</sup>Don’t strive with a man without cause,  
&emsp;&emsp;if he has done you no harm.  
<sup>31</sup>Don’t envy the man of violence.  
&emsp;&emsp;Choose none of his ways.  
<sup>32</sup>For the perverse is an abomination to the LORD,  
&emsp;&emsp;but his friendship is with the upright.  
<sup>33</sup>The LORD’s curse is in the house of the wicked,  
&emsp;&emsp;but he blesses the habitation of the righteous.  
<sup>34</sup>Surely he mocks the mockers,  
&emsp;&emsp;but he gives grace to the humble.  
<sup>35</sup>The wise will inherit glory,  
&emsp;&emsp;but shame will be the promotion of fools.  

## Proverbs 4

> **Date written:** c. 950 BC (Solomon) or c. 500–300 BC (critical)
>
> Part of the introductory instruction (chs. 1–9); see chapter 1 for the dating question.
>
> **Basis:**
> - [Solomon](../reference/anchors.md#solomon), c. 970–931 BC. Forty years (1 Kgs 11:42). The temple was begun in his 4th year (967/966 BC, 1 Kgs 6:1) and finished seven years later (6:38).
>
> **Sources:** [Thiele 1983](../reference/sources.md#thiele); [Galil 1996](../reference/sources.md#galil); [Shoshenq I relief](../reference/sources.md#shoshenq-relief); [Cogan; Cogan & Tadmor](../reference/sources.md#cogan-kings).

<sup>1</sup>Listen, sons, to a father’s instruction.  
&emsp;&emsp;Pay attention and know understanding;  
<sup>2</sup>for I give you sound learning.  
&emsp;&emsp;Don’t forsake my law.  
<sup>3</sup>For I was a son to my father,  
&emsp;&emsp;tender and an only child in the sight of my mother.  
<sup>4</sup>He taught me, and said to me:  
&emsp;&emsp;“Let your heart retain my words.  
&emsp;&emsp;Keep my commandments, and live.  
<sup>5</sup>Get wisdom.  
&emsp;&emsp;Get understanding.  
&emsp;&emsp;Don’t forget, and don’t deviate from the words of my mouth.  
<sup>6</sup>Don’t forsake her, and she will preserve you.  
&emsp;&emsp;Love her, and she will keep you.  
<sup>7</sup>Wisdom is supreme.  
&emsp;&emsp;Get wisdom.  
&emsp;&emsp;Yes, though it costs all your possessions, get understanding.  
<sup>8</sup>Esteem her, and she will exalt you.  
&emsp;&emsp;She will bring you to honor when you embrace her.  
<sup>9</sup>She will give to your head a garland of grace.  
&emsp;&emsp;She will deliver a crown of splendor to you.”  
<sup>10</sup>Listen, my son, and receive my sayings.  
&emsp;&emsp;The years of your life will be many.  
<sup>11</sup>I have taught you in the way of wisdom.  
&emsp;&emsp;I have led you in straight paths.  
<sup>12</sup>When you go, your steps will not be hampered.  
&emsp;&emsp;When you run, you will not stumble.  
<sup>13</sup>Take firm hold of instruction.  
&emsp;&emsp;Don’t let her go.  
&emsp;&emsp;Keep her, for she is your life.  
<sup>14</sup>Don’t enter into the path of the wicked.  
&emsp;&emsp;Don’t walk in the way of evil men.  
<sup>15</sup>Avoid it, and don’t pass by it.  
&emsp;&emsp;Turn from it, and pass on.  
<sup>16</sup>For they don’t sleep unless they do evil.  
&emsp;&emsp;Their sleep is taken away, unless they make someone fall.  
<sup>17</sup>For they eat the bread of wickedness  
&emsp;&emsp;and drink the wine of violence.  
<sup>18</sup>But the path of the righteous is like the dawning light  
&emsp;&emsp;that shines more and more until the perfect day.  
<sup>19</sup>The way of the wicked is like darkness.  
&emsp;&emsp;They don’t know what they stumble over.  
<sup>20</sup>My son, attend to my words.  
&emsp;&emsp;Turn your ear to my sayings.  
<sup>21</sup>Let them not depart from your eyes.  
&emsp;&emsp;Keep them in the center of your heart.  
<sup>22</sup>For they are life to those who find them,  
&emsp;&emsp;and health to their whole body.  
<sup>23</sup>Keep your heart with all diligence,  
&emsp;&emsp;for out of it is the wellspring of life.  
<sup>24</sup>Put away from yourself a perverse mouth.  
&emsp;&emsp;Put corrupt lips far from you.  
<sup>25</sup>Let your eyes look straight ahead.  
&emsp;&emsp;Fix your gaze directly before you.  
<sup>26</sup>Make the path of your feet level.  
&emsp;&emsp;Let all of your ways be established.  
<sup>27</sup>Don’t turn to the right hand nor to the left.  
&emsp;&emsp;Remove your foot from evil.  

## Proverbs 5

> **Date written:** c. 950 BC (Solomon) or c. 500–300 BC (critical)
>
> Part of the introductory instruction (chs. 1–9); see chapter 1 for the dating question.
>
> **Basis:**
> - [Solomon](../reference/anchors.md#solomon), c. 970–931 BC. Forty years (1 Kgs 11:42). The temple was begun in his 4th year (967/966 BC, 1 Kgs 6:1) and finished seven years later (6:38).
>
> **Sources:** [Thiele 1983](../reference/sources.md#thiele); [Galil 1996](../reference/sources.md#galil); [Shoshenq I relief](../reference/sources.md#shoshenq-relief); [Cogan; Cogan & Tadmor](../reference/sources.md#cogan-kings).

<sup>1</sup>My son, pay attention to my wisdom.  
&emsp;&emsp;Turn your ear to my understanding,  
<sup>2</sup>that you may maintain discretion,  
&emsp;&emsp;that your lips may preserve knowledge.  
<sup>3</sup>For the lips of an adulteress drip honey.  
&emsp;&emsp;Her mouth is smoother than oil,  
<sup>4</sup>but in the end she is as bitter as wormwood,  
&emsp;&emsp;and as sharp as a two-edged sword.  
<sup>5</sup>Her feet go down to death.  
&emsp;&emsp;Her steps lead straight to Sheol.[^5-1]  
<sup>6</sup>She gives no thought to the way of life.  
&emsp;&emsp;Her ways are crooked, and she doesn’t know it.  
<sup>7</sup>Now therefore, my sons, listen to me.  
&emsp;&emsp;Don’t depart from the words of my mouth.  
<sup>8</sup>Remove your way far from her.  
&emsp;&emsp;Don’t come near the door of her house,  
<sup>9</sup>lest you give your honor to others,  
&emsp;&emsp;and your years to the cruel one;  
<sup>10</sup>lest strangers feast on your wealth,  
&emsp;&emsp;and your labors enrich another man’s house.  
<sup>11</sup>You will groan at your latter end,  
&emsp;&emsp;when your flesh and your body are consumed,  
<sup>12</sup>and say, “How I have hated instruction,  
&emsp;&emsp;and my heart despised reproof.  
<sup>13</sup>I haven’t obeyed the voice of my teachers,  
&emsp;&emsp;nor turned my ear to those who instructed me!  
<sup>14</sup>I have come to the brink of utter ruin,  
&emsp;&emsp;among the gathered assembly.”  
<sup>15</sup>Drink water out of your own cistern,  
&emsp;&emsp;running water out of your own well.  
<sup>16</sup>Should your springs overflow in the streets,  
&emsp;&emsp;streams of water in the public squares?  
<sup>17</sup>Let them be for yourself alone,  
&emsp;&emsp;not for strangers with you.  
<sup>18</sup>Let your spring be blessed.  
&emsp;&emsp;Rejoice in the wife of your youth.  
<sup>19</sup>A loving doe and a graceful deer—  
&emsp;&emsp;let her breasts satisfy you at all times.  
&emsp;&emsp;Be captivated always with her love.  
<sup>20</sup>For why should you, my son, be captivated with an adulteress?  
&emsp;&emsp;Why embrace the bosom of another?  
<sup>21</sup>For the ways of man are before the LORD’s eyes.  
&emsp;&emsp;He examines all his paths.  
<sup>22</sup>The evil deeds of the wicked ensnare him.  
&emsp;&emsp;The cords of his sin hold him firmly.  
<sup>23</sup>He will die for lack of instruction.  
&emsp;&emsp;In the greatness of his folly, he will go astray.  

[^5-1]: Sheol is the place of the dead.

## Proverbs 6

> **Date written:** c. 950 BC (Solomon) or c. 500–300 BC (critical)
>
> Part of the introductory instruction (chs. 1–9); see chapter 1 for the dating question.
>
> **Basis:**
> - [Solomon](../reference/anchors.md#solomon), c. 970–931 BC. Forty years (1 Kgs 11:42). The temple was begun in his 4th year (967/966 BC, 1 Kgs 6:1) and finished seven years later (6:38).
>
> **Sources:** [Thiele 1983](../reference/sources.md#thiele); [Galil 1996](../reference/sources.md#galil); [Shoshenq I relief](../reference/sources.md#shoshenq-relief); [Cogan; Cogan & Tadmor](../reference/sources.md#cogan-kings).

<sup>1</sup>My son, if you have become collateral for your neighbor,  
&emsp;&emsp;if you have struck your hands in pledge for a stranger,  
<sup>2</sup>you are trapped by the words of your mouth;  
&emsp;&emsp;you are ensnared with the words of your mouth.  
<sup>3</sup>Do this now, my son, and deliver yourself,  
&emsp;&emsp;since you have come into the hand of your neighbor.  
Go, humble yourself.  
&emsp;&emsp;Press your plea with your neighbor.  
<sup>4</sup>Give no sleep to your eyes,  
&emsp;&emsp;nor slumber to your eyelids.  
<sup>5</sup>Free yourself, like a gazelle from the hand of the hunter,  
&emsp;&emsp;like a bird from the snare of the fowler.  
<sup>6</sup>Go to the ant, you sluggard.  
&emsp;&emsp;Consider her ways, and be wise;  
<sup>7</sup>which having no chief, overseer, or ruler,  
&emsp;&emsp;<sup>8</sup>provides her bread in the summer,  
&emsp;&emsp;and gathers her food in the harvest.  
<sup>9</sup>How long will you sleep, sluggard?  
&emsp;&emsp;When will you arise out of your sleep?  
<sup>10</sup>A little sleep, a little slumber,  
&emsp;&emsp;a little folding of the hands to sleep—  
<sup>11</sup>so your poverty will come as a robber,

<sup>25</sup>Don’t lust after her beauty in your heart,

<sup>1</sup>My son, keep my words.

<sup>2</sup>Keep my commandments and live!  
&emsp;&emsp;Guard my teaching as the apple of your eye.  
<sup>3</sup>Bind them on your fingers.  
&emsp;&emsp;Write them on the tablet of your heart.  
<sup>4</sup>Tell wisdom, “You are my sister.”  
&emsp;&emsp;Call understanding your relative,

<sup>20</sup>He has taken a bag of money with him.

## Proverbs 8

> **Date written:** c. 950 BC (Solomon) or c. 500–300 BC (critical)
>
> Wisdom speaks as a woman present at creation (8:22–31). This personification is central to the late dating of chs. 1–9; it influenced later Jewish writings such as Sirach 24 and the Wisdom of Solomon.
>
> **Basis:**
> - [Solomon](../reference/anchors.md#solomon), c. 970–931 BC. Forty years (1 Kgs 11:42). The temple was begun in his 4th year (967/966 BC, 1 Kgs 6:1) and finished seven years later (6:38).
>
> **Sources:** [Sirach](../reference/sources.md#sirach); [Thiele 1983](../reference/sources.md#thiele); [Galil 1996](../reference/sources.md#galil); [Shoshenq I relief](../reference/sources.md#shoshenq-relief); [Cogan; Cogan & Tadmor](../reference/sources.md#cogan-kings).

<sup>1</sup>Doesn’t wisdom cry out?  
&emsp;&emsp;Doesn’t understanding raise her voice?  
<sup>2</sup>On the top of high places by the way,  
&emsp;&emsp;where the paths meet, she stands.  
<sup>3</sup>Beside the gates, at the entry of the city,  
&emsp;&emsp;at the entry doors, she cries aloud:  
<sup>4</sup>“I call to you men!  
&emsp;&emsp;I send my voice to the sons of mankind.  
<sup>5</sup>You simple, understand prudence!  
&emsp;&emsp;You fools, be of an understanding heart!  
<sup>6</sup>Hear, for I will speak excellent things.  
&emsp;&emsp;The opening of my lips is for right things.  
<sup>7</sup>For my mouth speaks truth.  
&emsp;&emsp;Wickedness is an abomination to my lips.  
<sup>8</sup>All the words of my mouth are in righteousness.  
&emsp;&emsp;There is nothing crooked or perverse in them.  
<sup>9</sup>They are all plain to him who understands,  
&emsp;&emsp;right to those who find knowledge.  
<sup>10</sup>Receive my instruction rather than silver,  
&emsp;&emsp;knowledge rather than choice gold.  
<sup>11</sup>For wisdom is better than rubies.  
&emsp;&emsp;All the things that may be desired can’t be compared to it.  
<sup>12</sup>“I, wisdom, have made prudence my dwelling.  
&emsp;&emsp;Find out knowledge and discretion.  
<sup>13</sup>The fear of the LORD is to hate evil.  
&emsp;&emsp;I hate pride, arrogance, the evil way, and the perverse mouth.  
<sup>14</sup>Counsel and sound knowledge are mine.  
&emsp;&emsp;I have understanding and power.  
<sup>15</sup>By me kings reign,  
&emsp;&emsp;and princes decree justice.  
<sup>16</sup>By me princes rule,  
&emsp;&emsp;nobles, and all the righteous rulers of the earth.  
<sup>17</sup>I love those who love me.  
&emsp;&emsp;Those who seek me diligently will find me.  
<sup>18</sup>With me are riches, honor,  
&emsp;&emsp;enduring wealth, and prosperity.  
<sup>19</sup>My fruit is better than gold, yes, than fine gold,  
&emsp;&emsp;my yield than choice silver.  
<sup>20</sup>I walk in the way of righteousness,  
&emsp;&emsp;in the middle of the paths of justice,  
<sup>21</sup>that I may give wealth to those who love me.  
&emsp;&emsp;I fill their treasuries.  
<sup>22</sup>“The LORD possessed me in the beginning of his work,  
&emsp;&emsp;before his deeds of old.  
<sup>23</sup>I was set up from everlasting, from the beginning,  
&emsp;&emsp;before the earth existed.  
<sup>24</sup>When there were no depths, I was born,  
&emsp;&emsp;when there were no springs abounding with water.  
<sup>25</sup>Before the mountains were settled in place,  
&emsp;&emsp;before the hills, I was born;  
<sup>26</sup>while as yet he had not made the earth, nor the fields,  
&emsp;&emsp;nor the beginning of the dust of the world.  
<sup>27</sup>When he established the heavens, I was there.  
&emsp;&emsp;When he set a circle on the surface of the deep,  
<sup>28</sup>when he established the clouds above,  
&emsp;&emsp;when the springs of the deep became strong,  
<sup>29</sup>when he gave to the sea its boundary,  
&emsp;&emsp;that the waters should not violate his commandment,  
&emsp;&emsp;when he marked out the foundations of the earth,  
<sup>30</sup>then I was the craftsman by his side.  
&emsp;&emsp;I was a delight day by day,  
&emsp;&emsp;always rejoicing before him,  
<sup>31</sup>rejoicing in his whole world.  
&emsp;&emsp;My delight was with the sons of men.  
<sup>32</sup>“Now therefore, my sons, listen to me,  
&emsp;&emsp;for blessed are those who keep my ways.  
<sup>33</sup>Hear instruction, and be wise.  
&emsp;&emsp;Don’t refuse it.  
<sup>34</sup>Blessed is the man who hears me,  
&emsp;&emsp;watching daily at my gates,  
&emsp;&emsp;waiting at my door posts.  
<sup>35</sup>For whoever finds me finds life,  
&emsp;&emsp;and will obtain favor from the LORD.  
<sup>36</sup>But he who sins against me wrongs his own soul.  
&emsp;&emsp;All those who hate me love death.”

## Proverbs 10

> **Date written:** c. 950–700 BC (Solomonic collection)
>
> A new heading: "The proverbs of Solomon" (10:1). The collection 10:1–22:16 is 375 two-line sayings, the numerical value of the name "Solomon" in Hebrew letters. Most scholars, critical ones included, regard it as the oldest part, from the monarchy.
>
> **Basis:**
> - [Solomon](../reference/anchors.md#solomon), c. 970–931 BC. Forty years (1 Kgs 11:42). The temple was begun in his 4th year (967/966 BC, 1 Kgs 6:1) and finished seven years later (6:38).
>
> **Sources:** [Thiele 1983](../reference/sources.md#thiele); [Galil 1996](../reference/sources.md#galil); [Shoshenq I relief](../reference/sources.md#shoshenq-relief); [Cogan; Cogan & Tadmor](../reference/sources.md#cogan-kings).

<sup>1</sup>The proverbs of Solomon.

A wise son makes a glad father;  
&emsp;&emsp;but a foolish son brings grief to his mother.  
<sup>2</sup>Treasures of wickedness profit nothing,  
&emsp;&emsp;but righteousness delivers from death.  
<sup>3</sup>The LORD will not allow the soul of the righteous to go hungry,  
&emsp;&emsp;but he thrusts away the desire of the wicked.  
<sup>4</sup>He becomes poor who works with a lazy hand,  
&emsp;&emsp;but the hand of the diligent brings wealth.  
<sup>5</sup>He who gathers in summer is a wise son,  
&emsp;&emsp;but he who sleeps during the harvest is a son who causes shame.  
<sup>6</sup>Blessings are on the head of the righteous,  
&emsp;&emsp;but violence covers the mouth of the wicked.  
<sup>7</sup>The memory of the righteous is blessed,  
&emsp;&emsp;but the name of the wicked will rot.  
<sup>8</sup>The wise in heart accept commandments,  
&emsp;&emsp;but a chattering fool will fall.  
<sup>9</sup>He who walks blamelessly walks surely,  
&emsp;&emsp;but he who perverts his ways will be found out.  
<sup>10</sup>One who winks with the eye causes sorrow,  
&emsp;&emsp;but a chattering fool will fall.  
<sup>11</sup>The mouth of the righteous is a spring of life,  
&emsp;&emsp;but violence covers the mouth of the wicked.  
<sup>12</sup>Hatred stirs up strife,  
&emsp;&emsp;but love covers all wrongs.  
<sup>13</sup>Wisdom is found on the lips of him who has discernment,  
&emsp;&emsp;but a rod is for the back of him who is void of understanding.  
<sup>14</sup>Wise men lay up knowledge,  
&emsp;&emsp;but the mouth of the foolish is near ruin.  
<sup>15</sup>The rich man’s wealth is his strong city.  
&emsp;&emsp;The destruction of the poor is their poverty.  
<sup>16</sup>The labor of the righteous leads to life.  
&emsp;&emsp;The increase of the wicked leads to sin.  
<sup>17</sup>He is in the way of life who heeds correction,  
&emsp;&emsp;but he who forsakes reproof leads others astray.  
<sup>18</sup>He who hides hatred has lying lips.  
&emsp;&emsp;He who utters a slander is a fool.  
<sup>19</sup>In the multitude of words there is no lack of disobedience,  
&emsp;&emsp;but he who restrains his lips does wisely.  
<sup>20</sup>The tongue of the righteous is like choice silver.  
&emsp;&emsp;The heart of the wicked is of little worth.  
<sup>21</sup>The lips of the righteous feed many,  
&emsp;&emsp;but the foolish die for lack of understanding.  
<sup>22</sup>The LORD’s blessing brings wealth,  
&emsp;&emsp;and he adds no trouble to it.  
<sup>23</sup>It is a fool’s pleasure to do wickedness,  
&emsp;&emsp;but wisdom is a man of understanding’s pleasure.  
<sup>24</sup>What the wicked fear will overtake them,  
&emsp;&emsp;but the desire of the righteous will be granted.  
<sup>25</sup>When the whirlwind passes, the wicked is no more;  
&emsp;&emsp;but the righteous stand firm forever.  
<sup>26</sup>As vinegar to the teeth, and as smoke to the eyes,  
&emsp;&emsp;so is the sluggard to those who send him.  
<sup>27</sup>The fear of the LORD prolongs days,  
&emsp;&emsp;but the years of the wicked shall be shortened.  
<sup>28</sup>The prospect of the righteous is joy,  
&emsp;&emsp;but the hope of the wicked will perish.  
<sup>29</sup>The way of the LORD is a stronghold to the upright,  
&emsp;&emsp;but it is a destruction to the workers of iniquity.  
<sup>30</sup>The righteous will never be removed,  
&emsp;&emsp;but the wicked will not dwell in the land.  
<sup>31</sup>The mouth of the righteous produces wisdom,  
&emsp;&emsp;but the perverse tongue will be cut off.  
<sup>32</sup>The lips of the righteous know what is acceptable,  
&emsp;&emsp;but the mouth of the wicked is perverse.

<sup>19</sup>Longing fulfilled is sweet to the soul,  
&emsp;&emsp;but fools detest turning from evil.  
<sup>20</sup>One who walks with wise men grows wise,

<sup>19</sup>The way of the sluggard is like a thorn patch,

<sup>20</sup>A wise son makes a father glad,  
&emsp;&emsp;but a foolish man despises his mother.  
<sup>21</sup>Folly is joy to one who is void of wisdom,  
&emsp;&emsp;but a man of understanding keeps his way straight.  
<sup>22</sup>Where there is no counsel, plans fail;

<sup>28</sup>The heart of the righteous weighs answers,

<sup>29</sup>The LORD is far from the wicked,

<sup>30</sup>The light of the eyes rejoices the heart.

<sup>31</sup>The ear that listens to reproof lives,

## Proverbs 18

>

>

## Proverbs 22

> **Date written:** c. 950–700 BC (Solomon; the words of the wise)
>
> The Solomonic collection ends at 22:16. "Turn your ear, and listen to the words of the wise" (22:17) opens a new collection whose "thirty sayings" (22:20) closely parallel the Egyptian *Instruction of Amenemope*, a thirty-chapter text known from copies of the early 1st millennium BC. Most scholars think Proverbs adapted Amenemope.
>
> **Basis:**
> - [Solomon](../reference/anchors.md#solomon), c. 970–931 BC. Forty years (1 Kgs 11:42). The temple was begun in his 4th year (967/966 BC, 1 Kgs 6:1) and finished seven years later (6:38).
>
> **Sources:** [ANET](../reference/sources.md#anet); [Thiele 1983](../reference/sources.md#thiele); [Galil 1996](../reference/sources.md#galil); [Shoshenq I relief](../reference/sources.md#shoshenq-relief); [Cogan; Cogan & Tadmor](../reference/sources.md#cogan-kings).

<sup>1</sup>A good name is more desirable than great riches,  
&emsp;&emsp;and loving favor is better than silver and gold.  
<sup>2</sup>The rich and the poor have this in common:  
&emsp;&emsp;The LORD is the maker of them all.  
<sup>3</sup>A prudent man sees danger and hides himself;  
&emsp;&emsp;but the simple pass on, and suffer for it.  
<sup>4</sup>The result of humility and the fear of the LORD  
&emsp;&emsp;is wealth, honor, and life.  
<sup>5</sup>Thorns and snares are in the path of the wicked;  
&emsp;&emsp;whoever guards his soul stays far from them.  
<sup>6</sup>Train up a child in the way he should go,  
&emsp;&emsp;and when he is old he will not depart from it.  
<sup>7</sup>The rich rule over the poor.  
&emsp;&emsp;The borrower is servant to the lender.  
<sup>8</sup>He who sows wickedness reaps trouble,  
&emsp;&emsp;and the rod of his fury will be destroyed.  
<sup>9</sup>He who has a generous eye will be blessed,  
&emsp;&emsp;for he shares his food with the poor.  
<sup>10</sup>Drive out the mocker, and strife will go out;  
&emsp;&emsp;yes, quarrels and insults will stop.  
<sup>11</sup>He who loves purity of heart and speaks gracefully  
&emsp;&emsp;is the king’s friend.  
<sup>12</sup>The LORD’s eyes watch over knowledge,  
&emsp;&emsp;but he frustrates the words of the unfaithful.  
<sup>13</sup>The sluggard says, “There is a lion outside!  
&emsp;&emsp;I will be killed in the streets!”  
<sup>14</sup>The mouth of an adulteress is a deep pit.  
&emsp;&emsp;He who is under the LORD’s wrath will fall into it.  
<sup>15</sup>Folly is bound up in the heart of a child;  
&emsp;&emsp;the rod of discipline drives it far from him.  
<sup>16</sup>Whoever oppresses the poor for his own increase and whoever gives to the rich,  
&emsp;&emsp;both come to poverty.  
<sup>17</sup>Turn your ear, and listen to the words of the wise.  
&emsp;&emsp;Apply your heart to my teaching.  
<sup>18</sup>For it is a pleasant thing if you keep them within you,  
&emsp;&emsp;if all of them are ready on your lips.  
<sup>19</sup>I teach you today, even you,  
&emsp;&emsp;so that your trust may be in the LORD.  
<sup>20</sup>Haven’t I written to you thirty excellent things  
&emsp;&emsp;of counsel and knowledge,  
<sup>21</sup>To teach you truth, reliable words,  
&emsp;&emsp;to give sound answers to the ones who sent you?  
<sup>22</sup>Don’t exploit the poor because he is poor;  
&emsp;&emsp;and don’t crush the needy in court;  
<sup>23</sup>for the LORD will plead their case,  
&emsp;&emsp;and plunder the life of those who plunder them.  
<sup>24</sup>Don’t befriend a hot-tempered man.  
&emsp;&emsp;Don’t associate with one who harbors anger,  
<sup>25</sup>lest you learn his ways  
&emsp;&emsp;and ensnare your soul.  
<sup>26</sup>Don’t you be one of those who strike hands,  
&emsp;&emsp;of those who are collateral for debts.  
<sup>27</sup>If you don’t have means to pay,  
&emsp;&emsp;why should he take away your bed from under you?  
<sup>28</sup>Don’t move the ancient boundary stone  
&emsp;&emsp;which your fathers have set up.  
<sup>29</sup>Do you see a man skilled in his work?  
&emsp;&emsp;He will serve kings.  
&emsp;&emsp;He won’t serve obscure men.  

## Proverbs 23

> **Date written:** c. 1000–700 BC (the words of the wise)
>
> The words of the wise (22:17–24:22). The Amenemope parallels continue to about 23:11; the date is the monarchy, when Judah's court had contact with Egyptian scribal culture.
>
> **Sources:** [ANET](../reference/sources.md#anet).

<sup>1</sup>When you sit to eat with a ruler,  
&emsp;&emsp;consider diligently what is before you;  
<sup>2</sup>put a knife to your throat  
&emsp;&emsp;if you are a man given to appetite.  
<sup>3</sup>Don’t be desirous of his dainties,  
&emsp;&emsp;since they are deceitful food.  
<sup>4</sup>Don’t weary yourself to be rich.  
&emsp;&emsp;In your wisdom, show restraint.  
<sup>5</sup>Why do you set your eyes on that which is not?  
&emsp;&emsp;For it certainly sprouts wings like an eagle and flies in the sky.  
<sup>6</sup>Don’t eat the food of him who has a stingy eye,  
&emsp;&emsp;and don’t crave his delicacies,  
&emsp;&emsp;<sup>7</sup>for as he thinks about the cost, so he is.  
&emsp;&emsp;“Eat and drink!” he says to you,  
&emsp;&emsp;but his heart is not with you.  
<sup>8</sup>You will vomit up the morsel which you have eaten  
&emsp;&emsp;and waste your pleasant words.  
<sup>9</sup>Don’t speak in the ears of a fool,  
&emsp;&emsp;for he will despise the wisdom of your words.  
<sup>10</sup>Don’t move the ancient boundary stone.  
&emsp;&emsp;Don’t encroach on the fields of the fatherless,  
<sup>11</sup>for their Defender is strong.  
&emsp;&emsp;He will plead their case against you.  
<sup>12</sup>Apply your heart to instruction,  
&emsp;&emsp;and your ears to the words of knowledge.  
<sup>13</sup>Don’t withhold correction from a child.  
&emsp;&emsp;If you punish him with the rod, he will not die.  
<sup>14</sup>Punish him with the rod,  
&emsp;&emsp;and save his soul from Sheol.[^23-1]  
<sup>15</sup>My son, if your heart is wise,  
&emsp;&emsp;then my heart will be glad, even mine.  
<sup>16</sup>Yes, my heart will rejoice  
&emsp;&emsp;when your lips speak what is right.  
<sup>17</sup>Don’t let your heart envy sinners,  
&emsp;&emsp;but rather fear the LORD all day long.  
<sup>18</sup>Indeed surely there is a future hope,  
&emsp;&emsp;and your hope will not be cut off.

<sup>26</sup>My son, give me your heart;

&emsp;&emsp;When will I wake up? I can do it again.  
&emsp;&emsp;I will look for more.”

## Proverbs 24

> **Date written:** c. 1000–700 BC (the words of the wise)
>
> The words of the wise end at 24:22; "These also are sayings of the wise" (24:23) adds a short appendix (24:23–34), with no date of its own.

<sup>1</sup>Don’t be envious of evil men,  
&emsp;&emsp;neither desire to be with them;  
<sup>2</sup>for their hearts plot violence  
&emsp;&emsp;and their lips talk about mischief.  
<sup>3</sup>Through wisdom a house is built;  
&emsp;&emsp;by understanding it is established;  
<sup>4</sup>by knowledge the rooms are filled  
&emsp;&emsp;with all rare and beautiful treasure.  
<sup>5</sup>A wise man has great power.  
&emsp;&emsp;A knowledgeable man increases strength,  
<sup>6</sup>for by wise guidance you wage your war,  
&emsp;&emsp;and victory is in many advisors.  
<sup>7</sup>Wisdom is too high for a fool.  
&emsp;&emsp;He doesn’t open his mouth in the gate.  
<sup>8</sup>One who plots to do evil  
&emsp;&emsp;will be called a schemer.  
<sup>9</sup>The schemes of folly are sin.  
&emsp;&emsp;The mocker is detested by men.  
<sup>10</sup>If you falter in the time of trouble,  
&emsp;&emsp;your strength is small.  
<sup>11</sup>Rescue those who are being led away to death!  
&emsp;&emsp;Indeed, hold back those who are staggering to the slaughter!  
<sup>12</sup>If you say, “Behold, we didn’t know this,”  
&emsp;&emsp;doesn’t he who weighs the hearts consider it?  
He who keeps your soul, doesn’t he know it?  
&emsp;&emsp;Shall he not give to every man according to his work?  
<sup>13</sup>My son, eat honey, for it is good,  
&emsp;&emsp;the droppings of the honeycomb, which are sweet to your taste;  
<sup>14</sup>so you shall know wisdom to be to your soul.  
&emsp;&emsp;If you have found it, then there will be a reward:  
&emsp;&emsp;Your hope will not be cut off.  
<sup>15</sup>Don’t lay in wait, wicked man, against the habitation of the righteous.  
&emsp;&emsp;Don’t destroy his resting place;  
<sup>16</sup>for a righteous man falls seven times and rises up again,  
&emsp;&emsp;but the wicked are overthrown by calamity.  
<sup>17</sup>Don’t rejoice when your enemy falls.  
&emsp;&emsp;Don’t let your heart be glad when he is overthrown,  
<sup>18</sup>lest the LORD see it, and it displease him,  
&emsp;&emsp;and he turn away his wrath from him.  
<sup>19</sup>Don’t fret yourself because of evildoers,  
&emsp;&emsp;neither be envious of the wicked;  
<sup>20</sup>for there will be no reward to the evil man.  
&emsp;&emsp;The lamp of the wicked will be snuffed out.  
<sup>21</sup>My son, fear the LORD and the king.  
&emsp;&emsp;Don’t join those who are rebellious,  
<sup>22</sup>for their calamity will rise suddenly.  
&emsp;&emsp;Who knows what destruction may come from them both?  

<sup>23</sup>These also are sayings of the wise:

To show partiality in judgment is not good.  
<sup>24</sup>He who says to the wicked, “You are righteous,”  
&emsp;&emsp;peoples will curse him, and nations will abhor him—  
<sup>25</sup>but it will go well with those who convict the guilty,  
&emsp;&emsp;and a rich blessing will come on them.  
<sup>26</sup>An honest answer  
&emsp;&emsp;is like a kiss on the lips.  
<sup>27</sup>Prepare your work outside,  
&emsp;&emsp;and get your fields ready.  
&emsp;&emsp;Afterwards, build your house.  
<sup>28</sup>Don’t be a witness against your neighbor without cause.  
&emsp;&emsp;Don’t deceive with your lips.  
<sup>29</sup>Don’t say, “I will do to him as he has done to me;  
&emsp;&emsp;I will repay the man according to his work.”  
<sup>30</sup>I went by the field of the sluggard,  
&emsp;&emsp;by the vineyard of the man void of understanding.  
<sup>31</sup>Behold, it was all grown over with thorns.  
&emsp;&emsp;Its surface was covered with nettles,  
&emsp;&emsp;and its stone wall was broken down.  
<sup>32</sup>Then I saw, and considered well.  
&emsp;&emsp;I saw, and received instruction:  
<sup>33</sup>a little sleep, a little slumber,  
&emsp;&emsp;a little folding of the hands to sleep,  
<sup>34</sup>so your poverty will come as a robber  
&emsp;&emsp;and your want as an armed man.  

## Proverbs 25

> **Date written:** c. 715–686 BC (copied under Hezekiah)
>
> "These also are proverbs of Solomon, which the men of Hezekiah king of Judah copied out" (25:1): a second Solomonic collection (chs. 25–29), edited by royal scribes during Hezekiah's reign (Thiele 716/715–687/686 BC; earlier on a co-regency). The sayings themselves are older.
>
> **Basis:**
> - [Hezekiah and Sennacherib](../reference/anchors.md#hezekiah-701), Hezekiah 716/715–687/686 BC; invasion 701 BC. Sennacherib invaded Judah in 701 BC, "Hezekiah's 14th year" (2 Kgs 18:13), which gives Hezekiah's accession in 716/715 BC (Thiele). His annals describe shutting Hezekiah in Jerusalem "like a bird in a cage".
> - [Solomon](../reference/anchors.md#solomon), c. 970–931 BC. Forty years (1 Kgs 11:42). The temple was begun in his 4th year (967/966 BC, 1 Kgs 6:1) and finished seven years later (6:38).
>
> **Sources:** [Sennacherib's annals](../reference/sources.md#sennacherib-annals); [Thiele 1983](../reference/sources.md#thiele); [Galil 1996](../reference/sources.md#galil); [Cogan; Cogan & Tadmor](../reference/sources.md#cogan-kings); [Shoshenq I relief](../reference/sources.md#shoshenq-relief).

<sup>1</sup>These also are proverbs of Solomon, which the men of Hezekiah king of Judah copied out.

<sup>2</sup>It is the glory of God to conceal a thing,  
&emsp;&emsp;but the glory of kings is to search out a matter.  
<sup>3</sup>As the heavens for height, and the earth for depth,  
&emsp;&emsp;so the hearts of kings are unsearchable.  
<sup>4</sup>Take away the dross from the silver,  
&emsp;&emsp;and material comes out for the refiner.  
<sup>5</sup>Take away the wicked from the king’s presence,  
&emsp;&emsp;and his throne will be established in righteousness.  
<sup>6</sup>Don’t exalt yourself in the presence of the king,  
&emsp;&emsp;or claim a place among great men;  
<sup>7</sup>for it is better that it be said to you, “Come up here,”  
&emsp;&emsp;than that you should be put lower in the presence of the prince,  
&emsp;&emsp;whom your eyes have seen.  
<sup>8</sup>Don’t be hasty in bringing charges to court.  
&emsp;&emsp;What will you do in the end when your neighbor shames you?  
<sup>9</sup>Debate your case with your neighbor,  
&emsp;&emsp;and don’t betray the confidence of another,  
&emsp;&emsp;<sup>10</sup>lest one who hears it put you to shame,  
&emsp;&emsp;and your bad reputation never depart.  
<sup>11</sup>A word fitly spoken  
&emsp;&emsp;is like apples of gold in settings of silver.  
<sup>12</sup>As an earring of gold, and an ornament of fine gold,  
&emsp;&emsp;so is a wise reprover to an obedient ear.  
<sup>13</sup>As the cold of snow in the time of harvest,  
&emsp;&emsp;so is a faithful messenger to those who send him;  
&emsp;&emsp;for he refreshes the soul of his masters.  
<sup>14</sup>As clouds and wind without rain,  
&emsp;&emsp;so is he who boasts of gifts deceptively.  
<sup>15</sup>By patience a ruler is persuaded.  
&emsp;&emsp;A soft tongue breaks the bone.  
<sup>16</sup>Have you found honey?  
&emsp;&emsp;Eat as much as is sufficient for you,  
&emsp;&emsp;lest you eat too much, and vomit it.  
<sup>17</sup>Let your foot be seldom in your neighbor’s house,  
&emsp;&emsp;lest he be weary of you, and hate you.  
<sup>18</sup>A man who gives false testimony against his neighbor  
&emsp;&emsp;is like a club, a sword, or a sharp arrow.  
<sup>19</sup>Confidence in someone unfaithful in time of trouble  
&emsp;&emsp;is like a bad tooth or a lame foot.  
<sup>20</sup>As one who takes away a garment in cold weather,  
&emsp;&emsp;or vinegar on soda,  
&emsp;&emsp;so is one who sings songs to a heavy heart.  
<sup>21</sup>If your enemy is hungry, give him food to eat.  
&emsp;&emsp;If he is thirsty, give him water to drink;  
<sup>22</sup>for you will heap coals of fire on his head,  
&emsp;&emsp;and the LORD will reward you.  
<sup>23</sup>The north wind produces rain;  
&emsp;&emsp;so a backbiting tongue brings an angry face.  
<sup>24</sup>It is better to dwell in the corner of the housetop  
&emsp;&emsp;than to share a house with a contentious woman.  
<sup>25</sup>Like cold water to a thirsty soul,  
&emsp;&emsp;so is good news from a far country.  
<sup>26</sup>Like a muddied spring and a polluted well,  
&emsp;&emsp;so is a righteous man who gives way before the wicked.  
<sup>27</sup>It is not good to eat much honey,  
&emsp;&emsp;nor is it honorable to seek one’s own honor.  
<sup>28</sup>Like a city that is broken down and without walls  
&emsp;&emsp;is a man whose spirit is without restraint.  

## Proverbs 26

>
> **Basis:**
> - [Hezekiah and Sennacherib](../reference/anchors.md#hezekiah-701), Hezekiah 716/715–687/686 BC; invasion 701 BC. <sup>1</sup>Like snow in summer, and as rain in harvest,  
&emsp;&emsp;so honor is not fitting for a fool.  
<sup>2</sup>Like a fluttering sparrow,  
&emsp;&emsp;like a darting swallow,  
&emsp;&emsp;so the undeserved curse doesn’t come to rest.  
<sup>3</sup>A whip is for the horse,  
&emsp;&emsp;a bridle for the donkey,

<sup>8</sup>As one who binds a stone in a sling,  
&emsp;&emsp;so is he who gives honor to a fool.  
<sup>9</sup>Like a thorn bush that goes into the hand of a drunkard,

<sup>14</sup>As the door turns on its hinges,  
&emsp;&emsp;so does the sluggard on his bed.  
<sup>15</sup>The sluggard buries his hand in the dish.

<sup>16</sup>The sluggard is wiser in his own eyes  
&emsp;&emsp;than seven men who answer with discretion.  
<sup>17</sup>Like one who grabs a dog’s ears  
&emsp;&emsp;is one who passes by and meddles in a quarrel not his own.  
<sup>18</sup>Like a madman who shoots torches, arrows, and death,  
&emsp;&emsp;<sup>19</sup>is the man who deceives his neighbor and says, “Am I not joking?”  
<sup>20</sup>For lack of wood a fire goes out.  
&emsp;&emsp;Without gossip, a quarrel dies down.  
<sup>21</sup>As coals are to hot embers,  
&emsp;&emsp;and wood to fire,  
&emsp;&emsp;so is a contentious man to kindling strife.  
<sup>22</sup>The words of a whisperer are as dainty morsels,  
&emsp;&emsp;they go down into the innermost parts.  
<sup>23</sup>Like silver dross on an earthen vessel  
&emsp;&emsp;are the lips of a fervent one with an evil heart.  
<sup>24</sup>A malicious man disguises himself with his lips,  
&emsp;&emsp;but he harbors evil in his heart.  
<sup>25</sup>When his speech is charming, don’t believe him,  
&emsp;&emsp;for there are seven abominations in his heart.  
<sup>26</sup>His malice may be concealed by deception,  
&emsp;&emsp;but his wickedness will be exposed in the assembly.  
<sup>27</sup>Whoever digs a pit shall fall into it.  
&emsp;&emsp;Whoever rolls a stone, it will come back on him.  
<sup>28</sup>A lying tongue hates those it hurts;

<sup>1</sup>Don’t boast about tomorrow;  
&emsp;&emsp;for you don’t know what a day may bring.  
<sup>2</sup>Let another man praise you,  
&emsp;&emsp;and not your own mouth;

<sup>17</sup>Iron sharpens iron;  
&emsp;&emsp;so a man sharpens his friend’s countenance.  
<sup>18</sup>Whoever tends the fig tree shall eat its fruit.

<sup>19</sup>Like water reflects a face,

<sup>20</sup>Sheol[^27-1] and Abaddon are never satisfied;

<sup>21</sup>The crucible is for silver,

<sup>22</sup>Though you grind a fool in a mortar with a pestle along with grain,

<sup>23</sup>Know well the state of your flocks,

<sup>27</sup>There will be plenty of goats’ milk for your food,

## Proverbs 28

> **Date written:** c. 715–686 BC (copied under Hezekiah)
>
> Part of the collection copied by Hezekiah's men (chs. 25–29).
>
> **Basis:**
> - [Hezekiah and Sennacherib](../reference/anchors.md#hezekiah-701), Hezekiah 716/715–687/686 BC; invasion 701 BC. Sennacherib invaded Judah in 701 BC, "Hezekiah's 14th year" (2 Kgs 18:13), which gives Hezekiah's accession in 716/715 BC (Thiele). His annals describe shutting Hezekiah in Jerusalem "like a bird in a cage".

> **Sources:** [Sennacherib's annals](../reference/sources.md#sennacherib-annals); [Thiele 1983](../reference/sources.md#thiele); [Galil 1996](../reference/sources.md#galil); [Cogan; Cogan & Tadmor](../reference/sources.md#cogan-kings).

<sup>1</sup>The wicked flee when no one pursues;  
&emsp;&emsp;but the righteous are as bold as a lion.  
<sup>2</sup>In rebellion, a land has many rulers,  
&emsp;&emsp;but order is maintained by a man of understanding and knowledge.  
<sup>3</sup>A needy man who oppresses the poor  
&emsp;&emsp;is like a driving rain which leaves no crops.  
<sup>4</sup>Those who forsake the law praise the wicked;  
&emsp;&emsp;but those who keep the law contend with them.  
<sup>5</sup>Evil men don’t understand justice;  
&emsp;&emsp;but those who seek the LORD understand it fully.  
<sup>6</sup>Better is the poor who walks in his integrity  
&emsp;&emsp;than he who is perverse in his ways, and he is rich.  
<sup>7</sup>Whoever keeps the law is a wise son;  
&emsp;&emsp;but he who is a companion of gluttons shames his father.  
<sup>8</sup>He who increases his wealth by excessive interest  
&emsp;&emsp;gathers it for one who has pity on the poor.  
<sup>9</sup>He who turns away his ear from hearing the law,  
&emsp;&emsp;even his prayer is an abomination.  
<sup>10</sup>Whoever causes the upright to go astray in an evil way,  
&emsp;&emsp;he will fall into his own trap;  
&emsp;&emsp;but the blameless will inherit good.  
<sup>11</sup>The rich man is wise in his own eyes;

<sup>12</sup>When the righteous triumph, there is great glory;  
&emsp;&emsp;but when the wicked rise, men hide themselves.  
<sup>13</sup>He who conceals his sins doesn’t prosper,  
&emsp;&emsp;but whoever confesses and renounces them finds mercy.  
<sup>14</sup>Blessed is the man who always fears;  
&emsp;&emsp;but one who hardens his heart falls into trouble.  
<sup>15</sup>As a roaring lion or a charging bear,  
&emsp;&emsp;so is a wicked ruler over helpless people.  
<sup>16</sup>A tyrannical ruler lacks judgment.  
&emsp;&emsp;One who hates ill-gotten gain will have long days.  
<sup>17</sup>A man who is tormented by blood guilt will be a fugitive until death.  
&emsp;&emsp;No one will support him.  
<sup>18</sup>Whoever walks blamelessly is kept safe;  
&emsp;&emsp;but one with perverse ways will fall suddenly.  
<sup>19</sup>One who works his land will have an abundance of food;  
&emsp;&emsp;but one who chases fantasies will have his fill of poverty.  
<sup>20</sup>A faithful man is rich with blessings;  
&emsp;&emsp;but one who is eager to be rich will not go unpunished.  
<sup>21</sup>To show partiality is not good,  
&emsp;&emsp;yet a man will do wrong for a piece of bread.  
<sup>22</sup>A stingy man hurries after riches,  
&emsp;&emsp;and doesn’t know that poverty waits for him.  
<sup>23</sup>One who rebukes a man will afterward find more favor  
&emsp;&emsp;than one who flatters with the tongue.  
<sup>24</sup>Whoever robs his father or his mother and says, “It’s not wrong,”  
&emsp;&emsp;is a partner with a destroyer.  
<sup>25</sup>One who is greedy stirs up strife;  
&emsp;&emsp;but one who trusts in the LORD will prosper.  
<sup>26</sup>One who trusts in himself is a fool;  
&emsp;&emsp;but one who walks in wisdom is kept safe.  
<sup>27</sup>One who gives to the poor has no lack;  
&emsp;&emsp;but one who closes his eyes will have many curses.  
<sup>28</sup>When the wicked rise, men hide themselves;  
&emsp;&emsp;but when they perish, the righteous thrive.  

## Proverbs 29

> **Date written:** c. 715–686 BC (copied under Hezekiah)
>
> Part of the collection copied by Hezekiah's men (chs. 25–29).
>
> **Basis:**
> - [Hezekiah and Sennacherib](../reference/anchors.md#hezekiah-701), Hezekiah 716/715–687/686 BC; invasion 701 BC. Sennacherib invaded Judah in 701 BC, "Hezekiah's 14th year" (2 Kgs 18:13), which gives Hezekiah's accession in 716/715 BC (Thiele). His annals describe shutting Hezekiah in Jerusalem "like a bird in a cage".
>
> **Sources:** [Sennacherib's annals](../reference/sources.md#sennacherib-annals); [Thiele 1983](../reference/sources.md#thiele); [Galil 1996](../reference/sources.md#galil); [Cogan; Cogan & Tadmor](../reference/sources.md#cogan-kings).

<sup>1</sup>He who is often rebuked and stiffens his neck  
&emsp;&emsp;will be destroyed suddenly, with no remedy.  
<sup>2</sup>When the righteous thrive, the people rejoice;  
&emsp;&emsp;but when the wicked rule, the people groan.  
<sup>3</sup>Whoever loves wisdom brings joy to his father;  
&emsp;&emsp;but a companion of prostitutes squanders his wealth.  
<sup>4</sup>The king by justice makes the land stable,  
&emsp;&emsp;but he who takes bribes tears it down.  
<sup>5</sup>A man who flatters his neighbor  
&emsp;&emsp;spreads a net for his feet.  
<sup>6</sup>An evil man is snared by his sin,  
&emsp;&emsp;but the righteous can sing and be glad.  
<sup>7</sup>The righteous care about justice for the poor.  
&emsp;&emsp;The wicked aren’t concerned about knowledge.  
<sup>8</sup>Mockers stir up a city,  
&emsp;&emsp;but wise men turn away anger.  
<sup>9</sup>If a wise man goes to court with a foolish man,  
&emsp;&emsp;the fool rages or scoffs, and there is no peace.  
<sup>10</sup>The bloodthirsty hate a man of integrity;  
&emsp;&emsp;and they seek the life of the upright.  
<sup>11</sup>A fool vents all of his anger,  
&emsp;&emsp;but a wise man brings himself under control.  
<sup>12</sup>If a ruler listens to lies,  
&emsp;&emsp;all of his officials are wicked.  
<sup>13</sup>The poor man and the oppressor have this in common:  
&emsp;&emsp;The LORD gives sight to the eyes of both.  
<sup>14</sup>The king who fairly judges the poor,  
&emsp;&emsp;his throne shall be established forever.  
<sup>15</sup>The rod of correction gives wisdom,  
&emsp;&emsp;but a child left to himself causes shame to his mother.  
<sup>16</sup>When the wicked increase, sin increases;  
&emsp;&emsp;but the righteous will see their downfall.  
<sup>17</sup>Correct your son, and he will give you peace;  
&emsp;&emsp;yes, he will bring delight to your soul.  
<sup>18</sup>Where there is no revelation, the people cast off restraint;  
&emsp;&emsp;but one who keeps the law is blessed.  
<sup>19</sup>A servant can’t be corrected by words.  
&emsp;&emsp;Though he understands, yet he will not respond.  
<sup>20</sup>Do you see a man who is hasty in his words?  
&emsp;&emsp;There is more hope for a fool than for him.  
<sup>21</sup>He who pampers his servant from youth  
&emsp;&emsp;will have him become a son in the end.  
<sup>22</sup>An angry man stirs up strife,  
&emsp;&emsp;and a wrathful man abounds in sin.  
<sup>23</sup>A man’s pride brings him low,  
&emsp;&emsp;but one of lowly spirit gains honor.  
<sup>24</sup>Whoever is an accomplice of a thief is an enemy of his own soul.  
&emsp;&emsp;He takes an oath, but dares not testify.  
<sup>25</sup>The fear of man proves to be a snare,  
&emsp;&emsp;but whoever puts his trust in the LORD is kept safe.  
<sup>26</sup>Many seek the ruler’s favor,  
&emsp;&emsp;but a man’s justice comes from the LORD.  
<sup>27</sup>A dishonest man detests the righteous,  
&emsp;&emsp;and the upright in their ways detest the wicked.  

## Proverbs 30

> **Date written:** Undated (Agur); before c. 300 BC
>
> "The words of Agur the son of Jakeh, the revelation" (30:1). "The revelation" may be read *Massa*, an Arabian tribe (Gen 25:14), making Agur a non-Israelite sage. Nothing else is known of him. Numerical sayings ("three things ... yes, four", 30:15–31). The Septuagint places this chapter in a different part of the book, a sign that the appendices were arranged late.

<sup>1</sup>The words of Agur the son of Jakeh, the revelation:

the man says to Ithiel,  
&emsp;&emsp;to Ithiel and Ucal:  
<sup>2</sup>“Surely I am the most ignorant man,  
&emsp;&emsp;and don’t have a man’s understanding.  
<sup>3</sup>I have not learned wisdom,  
&emsp;&emsp;neither do I have the knowledge of the Holy One.  
<sup>4</sup>Who has ascended up into heaven, and descended?  
&emsp;&emsp;Who has gathered the wind in his fists?  
&emsp;&emsp;Who has bound the waters in his garment?  
&emsp;&emsp;Who has established all the ends of the earth?  
&emsp;&emsp;What is his name, and what is his son’s name, if you know?  
<sup>5</sup>“Every word of God is flawless.  
&emsp;&emsp;He is a shield to those who take refuge in him.  
<sup>6</sup>Don’t you add to his words,  
&emsp;&emsp;lest he reprove you, and you be found a liar.  
<sup>7</sup>“Two things I have asked of you.  
&emsp;&emsp;Don’t deny me before I die.  
<sup>8</sup>Remove far from me falsehood and lies.  
&emsp;&emsp;Give me neither poverty nor riches.  
&emsp;&emsp;Feed me with the food that is needful for me,  
<sup>9</sup>lest I be full, deny you, and say, ‘Who is the LORD?’  
&emsp;&emsp;or lest I be poor, and steal,  
&emsp;&emsp;and so dishonor the name of my God.  
<sup>10</sup>“Don’t slander a servant to his master,  
&emsp;&emsp;lest he curse you, and you be held guilty.  
<sup>11</sup>There is a generation that curses their father,  
&emsp;&emsp;and doesn’t bless their mother.  
<sup>12</sup>There is a generation that is pure in their own eyes,  
&emsp;&emsp;yet are not washed from their filthiness.  
<sup>13</sup>There is a generation, oh how lofty are their eyes!  
&emsp;&emsp;Their eyelids are lifted up.  
<sup>14</sup>There is a generation whose teeth are like swords,  
&emsp;&emsp;and their jaws like knives,  
&emsp;&emsp;to devour the poor from the earth, and the needy from among men.  
<sup>15</sup>“The leech has two daughters:  
&emsp;&emsp;‘Give, give.’  
“There are three things that are never satisfied;  
&emsp;&emsp;four that don’t say, ‘Enough!’:  
&emsp;&emsp;<sup>16</sup>Sheol,[^30-1]  
&emsp;&emsp;the barren womb,  
&emsp;&emsp;the earth that is not satisfied with water,  
&emsp;&emsp;and the fire that doesn’t say, ‘Enough!’  
<sup>17</sup>“The eye that mocks at his father,  
&emsp;&emsp;and scorns obedience to his mother,  
&emsp;&emsp;the ravens of the valley shall pick it out,  
&emsp;&emsp;the young eagles shall eat it.  
<sup>18</sup>“There are three things which are too amazing for me,  
&emsp;&emsp;four which I don’t understand:  
&emsp;&emsp;<sup>19</sup>The way of an eagle in the air,  
&emsp;&emsp;the way of a serpent on a rock,  
&emsp;&emsp;the way of a ship in the middle of the sea,  
&emsp;&emsp;and the way of a man with a maiden.  
<sup>20</sup>“So is the way of an adulterous woman:  
&emsp;&emsp;She eats and wipes her mouth,  
&emsp;&emsp;and says, ‘I have done nothing wrong.’  
<sup>21</sup>“For three things the earth trembles,  
&emsp;&emsp;and under four, it can’t bear up:  
&emsp;&emsp;<sup>22</sup>For a servant when he is king,  
&emsp;&emsp;a fool when he is filled with food,  
&emsp;&emsp;<sup>23</sup>for an unloved woman when she is married,  
&emsp;&emsp;and a servant who is heir to her mistress.  
<sup>24</sup>“There are four things which are little on the earth,  
&emsp;&emsp;but they are exceedingly wise:  
&emsp;&emsp;<sup>25</sup>The ants are not a strong people,  
&emsp;&emsp;yet they provide their food in the summer.  
&emsp;&emsp;<sup>26</sup>The hyraxes are but a feeble folk,  
&emsp;&emsp;yet make they their houses in the rocks.  
&emsp;&emsp;<sup>27</sup>The locusts have no king,  
&emsp;&emsp;yet they advance in ranks.  
&emsp;&emsp;<sup>28</sup>You can catch a lizard with your hands,  
&emsp;&emsp;yet it is in kings’ palaces.  
<sup>29</sup>“There are three things which are stately in their march,  
&emsp;&emsp;four which are stately in going:  
&emsp;&emsp;<sup>30</sup>The lion, which is mightiest among animals,  
&emsp;&emsp;and doesn’t turn away for any;  
&emsp;&emsp;<sup>31</sup>the greyhound;  
&emsp;&emsp;the male goat;  
&emsp;&emsp;and the king against whom there is no rising up.  
<sup>32</sup>“If you have done foolishly in lifting up yourself,  
&emsp;&emsp;or if you have thought evil,  
put your hand over your mouth.  
&emsp;&emsp;<sup>33</sup>For as the churning of milk produces butter,  
&emsp;&emsp;and the wringing of the nose produces blood,  
&emsp;&emsp;so the forcing of wrath produces strife.”  

[^30-1]: Sheol is the place of the dead.

## Proverbs 31

> **Date written:** Undated (Lemuel); 31:10–31 perhaps post-exilic
>
> "The words of King Lemuel, the revelation which his mother taught him" (31:1), again possibly "king of Massa"; Lemuel is otherwise unknown. The book closes with the acrostic poem on the capable wife (31:10–31), which many critical scholars date to the Persian period as part of the final edition.

<sup>1</sup>The words of King Lemuel—the revelation which his mother taught him:

<sup>2</sup>“Oh, my son!  
&emsp;&emsp;Oh, son of my womb!  
&emsp;&emsp;Oh, son of my vows!  
<sup>3</sup>Don’t give your strength to women,  
&emsp;&emsp;nor your ways to that which destroys kings.  
<sup>4</sup>It is not for kings, Lemuel,  
&emsp;&emsp;it is not for kings to drink wine,  
&emsp;&emsp;nor for princes to say, ‘Where is strong drink?’  
<sup>5</sup>lest they drink, and forget the law,  
&emsp;&emsp;and pervert the justice due to anyone who is afflicted.  
<sup>6</sup>Give strong drink to him who is ready to perish,  
&emsp;&emsp;and wine to the bitter in soul.  
<sup>7</sup>Let him drink, and forget his poverty,  
&emsp;&emsp;and remember his misery no more.  
<sup>8</sup>Open your mouth for the mute,  
&emsp;&emsp;in the cause of all who are left desolate.  
<sup>9</sup>Open your mouth, judge righteously,  
&emsp;&emsp;and serve justice to the poor and needy.”  
<sup>10</sup>[^31-1]Who can find a worthy woman?  
&emsp;&emsp;For her value is far above rubies.  
<sup>11</sup>The heart of her husband trusts in her.  
&emsp;&emsp;He shall have no lack of gain.  
<sup>12</sup>She does him good, and not harm,  
&emsp;&emsp;all the days of her life.  
<sup>13</sup>She seeks wool and flax,  
&emsp;&emsp;and works eagerly with her hands.  
<sup>14</sup>She is like the merchant ships.  
&emsp;&emsp;She brings her bread from afar.  
<sup>15</sup>She rises also while it is yet night,  
&emsp;&emsp;gives food to her household,  
&emsp;&emsp;and portions for her servant girls.  
<sup>16</sup>She considers a field, and buys it.  
&emsp;&emsp;With the fruit of her hands, she plants a vineyard.  
<sup>17</sup>She arms her waist with strength,  
&emsp;&emsp;and makes her arms strong.  
<sup>18</sup>She perceives that her merchandise is profitable.  
&emsp;&emsp;Her lamp doesn’t go out by night.  
<sup>19</sup>She lays her hands to the distaff,  
&emsp;&emsp;and her hands hold the spindle.  
<sup>20</sup>She opens her arms to the poor;  
&emsp;&emsp;yes, she extends her hands to the needy.  
<sup>21</sup>She is not afraid of the snow for her household,  
&emsp;&emsp;for all her household are clothed with scarlet.  
<sup>22</sup>She makes for herself carpets of tapestry.  
&emsp;&emsp;Her clothing is fine linen and purple.  
<sup>23</sup>Her husband is respected in the gates,  
&emsp;&emsp;when he sits among the elders of the land.  
<sup>24</sup>She makes linen garments and sells them,  
&emsp;&emsp;and delivers sashes to the merchant.  
<sup>25</sup>Strength and dignity are her clothing.  
&emsp;&emsp;She laughs at the time to come.  
<sup>26</sup>She opens her mouth with wisdom.  
&emsp;&emsp;Kind instruction is on her tongue.  
<sup>27</sup>She looks well to the ways of her household,  
&emsp;&emsp;and doesn’t eat the bread of idleness.  
<sup>28</sup>Her children rise up and call her blessed.  
&emsp;&emsp;Her husband also praises her:  
<sup>29</sup>“Many women do noble things,  
&emsp;&emsp;but you excel them all.”  
<sup>30</sup>Charm is deceitful, and beauty is vain;  
&emsp;&emsp;but a woman who fears the LORD, she shall be praised.  
<sup>31</sup>Give her of the fruit of her hands!  
&emsp;&emsp;Let her works praise her in the gates!  

[^31-1]: Proverbs 31:10-31 form an acrostic, with each verse starting with each letter of the Hebrew alphabet, in order.
