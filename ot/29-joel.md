# Joel

World English Bible text (public domain), with a date heading for each chapter.
See [How to read the date headings](README.md#how-to-read-the-date-headings).

<a id="date-of-writing"></a>

## Date of writing: Disputed: c. 835 BC (early view) or c. 500–350 BC (most scholars)

Joel has no superscription date (1:1 names only his father, Pethuel) and mentions no king. Dates proposed run from the 9th century to the 4th. Most critical scholars now put it after the exile, in the Persian period; an older conservative view put it in the minority of Joash of Judah (c. 835 BC).

| Date | Held by | Basis | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|
| c. 835–800 BC (Joash's minority) | Older conservative commentators; some conservative scholars today | Joel stands among the 8th-century prophets in the Hebrew order of the Twelve; no king is named because Joash was a boy ruled by the priest Jehoiada (2 Kgs 11–12), which would explain why priests and elders lead (1:13–14; 2:16–17); the enemies named (Tyre, Sidon, Philistia, Egypt, Edom, 3:4, 19) fit the 9th century; Amos 1:2 seems to echo Joel 3:16. | [Dillard & Longman](../reference/sources.md#dillard-longman); [Childs 1979](../reference/sources.md#childs) |
| c. 500–343 BC (Persian period) | Wolff (c. 445–343 BC); most critical scholars | The temple is standing and its offerings matter (1:9, 13; 2:14), and the city has walls (2:7, 9); there is no king and no northern kingdom; Judah has been "scattered among the nations" and its land divided (3:2); Judeans are sold "to the sons of the Greeks" (3:6). Joel quotes or alludes to many other prophets ("as the LORD has said", 2:32, cf. Obad 17). Sidon is still standing (3:4), so Wolff put the book before its destruction by Artaxerxes III in 343 BC. | [Wolff 1977](../reference/sources.md#wolff-joel-amos); [Collins, Hebrew Bible](../reference/sources.md#collins-intro) |
| Late 7th or early 6th century BC | Some scholars | The "Day of the LORD" and the enemy from the north (2:20) as in Zephaniah and Jeremiah; the locust army as an image of Babylon before 587 BC. | [Dillard & Longman](../reference/sources.md#dillard-longman) |

## Joel 1

> **Date of the prophecy:** c. 835 BC (early view) or c. 500–350 BC (post-exilic view)
>
> A locust plague and drought: "what the swarming locust has left, the great locust has eaten" (1:4). Grain and drink offerings are "cut off from the LORD's house" (1:9, 13), so the temple is functioning; priests, not a king, call the fast (1:13–14). Either a pre-exilic temple or the second temple (after 515 BC).
>
> **Sources:** [Wolff 1977](../reference/sources.md#wolff-joel-amos).
>
> **Book written:** Disputed: c. 835 BC (early view) or c. 500–350 BC (most scholars); see [the date of Joel](#date-of-writing).

<sup>1</sup>The LORD’s[^1-1] word that came to Joel, the son of Pethuel.

<sup>2</sup>Hear this, you elders,  
&emsp;&emsp;and listen, all you inhabitants of the land!  
Has this ever happened in your days,  
&emsp;&emsp;or in the days of your fathers?  
<sup>3</sup>Tell your children about it,  
&emsp;&emsp;and have your children tell their children,  
&emsp;&emsp;and their children, another generation.  
<sup>4</sup>What the swarming locust has left, the great locust has eaten.  
&emsp;&emsp;What the great locust has left, the grasshopper has eaten.  
&emsp;&emsp;What the grasshopper has left, the caterpillar has eaten.  
<sup>5</sup>Wake up, you drunkards, and weep!  
&emsp;&emsp;Wail, all you drinkers of wine, because of the sweet wine,  
&emsp;&emsp;for it is cut off from your mouth.  
<sup>6</sup>For a nation has come up on my land, strong, and without number.  
&emsp;&emsp;His teeth are the teeth of a lion,  
&emsp;&emsp;and he has the fangs of a lioness.  
<sup>7</sup>He has laid my vine waste,  
&emsp;&emsp;and stripped my fig tree.  
&emsp;&emsp;He has stripped its bark, and thrown it away.  
&emsp;&emsp;Its branches are made white.  
<sup>8</sup>Mourn like a virgin dressed in sackcloth  
&emsp;&emsp;for the husband of her youth!  
<sup>9</sup>The meal offering and the drink offering are cut off from the LORD’s house.  
&emsp;&emsp;The priests, the LORD’s ministers, mourn.  
<sup>10</sup>The field is laid waste.  
&emsp;&emsp;The land mourns, for the grain is destroyed,  
&emsp;&emsp;The new wine has dried up,  
&emsp;&emsp;and the oil languishes.  
<sup>11</sup>Be confounded, you farmers!  
&emsp;&emsp;Wail, you vineyard keepers,  
&emsp;&emsp;for the wheat and for the barley;  
&emsp;&emsp;for the harvest of the field has perished.  
<sup>12</sup>The vine has dried up, and the fig tree withered—  
&emsp;&emsp;the pomegranate tree, the palm tree also, and the apple tree,  
&emsp;&emsp;even all of the trees of the field are withered;  
&emsp;&emsp;for joy has withered away from the sons of men.  
<sup>13</sup>Put on sackcloth and mourn, you priests!  
&emsp;&emsp;Wail, you ministers of the altar.  
Come, lie all night in sackcloth, you ministers of my God,[^1-2]  
&emsp;&emsp;for the meal offering and the drink offering are withheld from your God’s house.  
<sup>14</sup>Sanctify a fast.  
&emsp;&emsp;Call a solemn assembly.  
&emsp;&emsp;Gather the elders and all the inhabitants of the land to the house of the LORD, your God,  
&emsp;&emsp;and cry to the LORD.  
<sup>15</sup>Alas for the day!  
&emsp;&emsp;For the day of the LORD is at hand,  
&emsp;&emsp;and it will come as destruction from the Almighty.  
<sup>16</sup>Isn’t the food cut off before our eyes,  
&emsp;&emsp;joy and gladness from the house of our God?  
<sup>17</sup>The seeds rot under their clods.  
&emsp;&emsp;The granaries are laid desolate.  
&emsp;&emsp;The barns are broken down, for the grain has withered.  
<sup>18</sup>How the animals groan!  
&emsp;&emsp;The herds of livestock are perplexed, because they have no pasture.  
&emsp;&emsp;Yes, the flocks of sheep are made desolate.  
<sup>19</sup>LORD, I cry to you,  
&emsp;&emsp;for the fire has devoured the pastures of the wilderness,  
&emsp;&emsp;and the flame has burned all the trees of the field.  
<sup>20</sup>Yes, the animals of the field pant to you,  
&emsp;&emsp;for the water brooks have dried up,  
&emsp;&emsp;and the fire has devoured the pastures of the wilderness.  

[^1-1]: When rendered in ALL CAPITAL LETTERS, “LORD” or “GOD” is the translation of God’s Proper Name (Hebrew “יהוה”, usually pronounced Yahweh).
[^1-2]: The Hebrew word rendered “God” is “אֱלֹהִ֑ים” (Elohim).

## Joel 2

> **Date of the prophecy:** c. 835 BC (early view) or c. 500–350 BC (post-exilic view)
>
> The locusts become an army of the Day of the LORD, running on the wall and climbing into houses (2:7–9); the people are called to return "with all your heart" (2:12). The promise "I will pour out my Spirit on all flesh" (2:28–32) is quoted by Peter at Pentecost (Acts 2:16–21). The city walls (2:9) are read by late-daters as Nehemiah's walls (rebuilt 445 BC).
>
> **Basis:**
> - [Ezra and Nehemiah](../reference/anchors.md#ezra-nehemiah), Ezra 458 BC (or 398 BC); Nehemiah 445–c. 430 BC. Nehemiah came in the 20th year of Artaxerxes I, 445 BC (Neh 2:1), and went back in his 32nd, 433 BC (13:6). Ezra came in "the 7th year of Artaxerxes" (Ezra 7:7): 458 BC if Artaxerxes I, the traditional order; 398 BC if Artaxerxes II, which would put Ezra after Nehemiah.
>
> **Sources:** [Wolff 1977](../reference/sources.md#wolff-joel-amos); [Elephantine papyri](../reference/sources.md#elephantine-papyri); [Williamson 1985](../reference/sources.md#williamson-ezra); [Parker & Dubberstein 1956](../reference/sources.md#parker-dubberstein); [Miller & Hayes 2006](../reference/sources.md#miller-hayes).
>
> **Book written:** Disputed: c. 835 BC (early view) or c. 500–350 BC (most scholars); see [the date of Joel](#date-of-writing).

<sup>1</sup>Blow the trumpet in Zion,  
&emsp;&emsp;and sound an alarm in my holy mountain!  
Let all the inhabitants of the land tremble,  
&emsp;&emsp;for the day of the LORD comes,  
&emsp;&emsp;for it is close at hand:  
<sup>2</sup>A day of darkness and gloominess,  
&emsp;&emsp;a day of clouds and thick darkness.  
As the dawn spreading on the mountains,  
&emsp;&emsp;a great and strong people;  
&emsp;&emsp;there has never been the like,  
&emsp;&emsp;neither will there be any more after them,  
&emsp;&emsp;even to the years of many generations.  
<sup>3</sup>A fire devours before them,  
&emsp;&emsp;and behind them, a flame burns.  
The land is as the garden of Eden before them,  
&emsp;&emsp;and behind them, a desolate wilderness.  
Yes, and no one has escaped them.  
<sup>4</sup>Their appearance is as the appearance of horses,  
&emsp;&emsp;and they run as horsemen.  
<sup>5</sup>Like the noise of chariots on the tops of the mountains, they leap,  
&emsp;&emsp;like the noise of a flame of fire that devours the stubble,  
&emsp;&emsp;like a strong people set in battle array.  
<sup>6</sup>At their presence the peoples are in anguish.  
&emsp;&emsp;All faces have grown pale.  
<sup>7</sup>They run like mighty men.  
&emsp;&emsp;They climb the wall like warriors.  
&emsp;&emsp;They each march in his line, and they don’t swerve off course.  
<sup>8</sup>One doesn’t jostle another.  
&emsp;&emsp;They each march in their own path.  
&emsp;&emsp;They burst through the defenses  
&emsp;&emsp;and don’t break ranks.  
<sup>9</sup>They rush on the city.  
&emsp;&emsp;They run on the wall.  
&emsp;&emsp;They climb up into the houses.  
&emsp;&emsp;They enter in at the windows like thieves.  
<sup>10</sup>The earth quakes before them.  
&emsp;&emsp;The heavens tremble.  
&emsp;&emsp;The sun and the moon are darkened,  
&emsp;&emsp;and the stars withdraw their shining.  
<sup>11</sup>The LORD thunders his voice before his army,  
&emsp;&emsp;for his forces are very great;  
&emsp;&emsp;for he is strong who obeys his command;  
&emsp;&emsp;for the day of the LORD is great and very awesome,  
&emsp;&emsp;and who can endure it?  
<sup>12</sup>“Yet even now,” says the LORD, “turn to me with all your heart,  
&emsp;&emsp;and with fasting, and with weeping, and with mourning.”  
<sup>13</sup>Tear your heart and not your garments,  
&emsp;&emsp;and turn to the LORD, your God;  
&emsp;&emsp;for he is gracious and merciful,  
&emsp;&emsp;slow to anger, and abundant in loving kindness,  
&emsp;&emsp;and relents from sending calamity.  
<sup>14</sup>Who knows? He may turn and relent,  
&emsp;&emsp;and leave a blessing behind him,  
&emsp;&emsp;even a meal offering and a drink offering to the LORD, your God.  
<sup>15</sup>Blow the trumpet in Zion!  
&emsp;&emsp;Sanctify a fast.  
&emsp;&emsp;Call a solemn assembly.  
<sup>16</sup>Gather the people.  
&emsp;&emsp;Sanctify the assembly.  
&emsp;&emsp;Assemble the elders.  
&emsp;&emsp;Gather the children, and those who nurse from breasts.  
Let the bridegroom go out of his room,  
&emsp;&emsp;and the bride out of her chamber.  
<sup>17</sup>Let the priests, the ministers of the LORD, weep between the porch and the altar,  
&emsp;&emsp;and let them say, “Spare your people, LORD,  
&emsp;&emsp;and don’t give your heritage to reproach,  
&emsp;&emsp;that the nations should rule over them.  
Why should they say among the peoples,  
&emsp;&emsp;‘Where is their God?’”  
<sup>18</sup>Then the LORD was jealous for his land,  
&emsp;&emsp;and had pity on his people.  
<sup>19</sup>The LORD answered his people,  
&emsp;&emsp;“Behold,[^2-1] I will send you grain, new wine, and oil,  
&emsp;&emsp;and you will be satisfied with them;  
&emsp;&emsp;and I will no more make you a reproach among the nations.  
<sup>20</sup>But I will remove the northern army far away from you,  
&emsp;&emsp;and will drive it into a barren and desolate land,  
&emsp;&emsp;its front into the eastern sea,  
&emsp;&emsp;and its back into the western sea;  
&emsp;&emsp;and its stench will come up,  
&emsp;&emsp;and its bad smell will rise.”  
Surely he has done great things.  
<sup>21</sup>Land, don’t be afraid.  
&emsp;&emsp;Be glad and rejoice, for the LORD has done great things.  
<sup>22</sup>Don’t be afraid, you animals of the field;  
&emsp;&emsp;for the pastures of the wilderness spring up,  
&emsp;&emsp;for the tree bears its fruit.  
&emsp;&emsp;The fig tree and the vine yield their strength.  
<sup>23</sup>“Be glad then, you children of Zion,  
&emsp;&emsp;and rejoice in the LORD, your God;  
&emsp;&emsp;for he gives you the early rain in just measure,  
&emsp;&emsp;and he causes the rain to come down for you,  
&emsp;&emsp;the early rain and the latter rain, as before.  
<sup>24</sup>The threshing floors will be full of wheat,  
&emsp;&emsp;and the vats will overflow with new wine and oil.  
<sup>25</sup>I will restore to you the years that the swarming locust has eaten,  
&emsp;&emsp;the great locust, the grasshopper, and the caterpillar,  
&emsp;&emsp;my great army, which I sent among you.  
<sup>26</sup>You will have plenty to eat and be satisfied,  
&emsp;&emsp;and will praise the name of the LORD, your God,  
&emsp;&emsp;who has dealt wondrously with you;  
&emsp;&emsp;and my people will never again be disappointed.  
<sup>27</sup>You will know that I am among Israel,  
&emsp;&emsp;and that I am the LORD, your God, and there is no one else;  
&emsp;&emsp;and my people will never again be disappointed.  
<sup>28</sup>“It will happen afterward, that I will pour out my Spirit on all flesh;  
&emsp;&emsp;and your sons and your daughters will prophesy.  
&emsp;&emsp;Your old men will dream dreams.  
&emsp;&emsp;Your young men will see visions.  
<sup>29</sup>And also on the servants and on the handmaids in those days,  
&emsp;&emsp;I will pour out my Spirit.  
<sup>30</sup>I will show wonders in the heavens and on the earth:  
&emsp;&emsp;blood, fire, and pillars of smoke.  
<sup>31</sup>The sun will be turned into darkness,  
&emsp;&emsp;and the moon into blood,  
&emsp;&emsp;before the great and terrible day of the LORD comes.  
<sup>32</sup>It will happen that whoever will call on the LORD’s name shall be saved;  
&emsp;&emsp;for in Mount Zion and in Jerusalem there will be those who escape,  
&emsp;&emsp;as the LORD has said,  
&emsp;&emsp;and among the remnant, those whom the LORD calls.  

[^2-1]: “Behold”, from “הִנֵּה”, means look at, take notice, observe, see, or gaze at. It is often used as an interjection.

## Joel 3

> **Date of the prophecy:** c. 835 BC (early view) or c. 500–350 BC (post-exilic view)
>
> Judgment in the valley of Jehoshaphat on the nations that "scattered" Israel and "divided my land" (3:2), and on Tyre, Sidon and Philistia, who sold Judeans "to the sons of the Greeks" (3:6). Late-daters read 3:2 as looking back on 587 BC; early-daters, as general language or prediction. Egypt and Edom are judged for violence against Judah (3:19).
>
> **Basis:**
> - [The Babylonian exile](../reference/anchors.md#exile), 597/587–539 BC. From the deportations of 597 and 587/586 BC to Cyrus's capture of Babylon in 539 BC. Jehoiachin was released by Evil-Merodach (Amel-Marduk) in 562 BC (2 Kgs 25:27). Ezekiel dates his oracles from Jehoiachin's exile (597 BC).
>
> **Sources:** [Wolff 1977](../reference/sources.md#wolff-joel-amos); [Parker & Dubberstein 1956](../reference/sources.md#parker-dubberstein); [Grayson 1975](../reference/sources.md#grayson-abc); [ANET](../reference/sources.md#anet); [Zimmerli 1979–83](../reference/sources.md#zimmerli).
>
> **Book written:** Disputed: c. 835 BC (early view) or c. 500–350 BC (most scholars); see [the date of Joel](#date-of-writing).

<sup>1</sup>“For, behold, in those days,  
&emsp;&emsp;and in that time,  
&emsp;&emsp;when I restore the fortunes of Judah and Jerusalem,  
<sup>2</sup>I will gather all nations,  
&emsp;&emsp;and will bring them down into the valley of Jehoshaphat;  
&emsp;&emsp;and I will execute judgment on them there for my people,  
&emsp;&emsp;and for my heritage, Israel, whom they have scattered among the nations.  
&emsp;&emsp;They have divided my land,  
&emsp;&emsp;<sup>3</sup>and have cast lots for my people,  
&emsp;&emsp;and have given a boy for a prostitute,  
&emsp;&emsp;and sold a girl for wine, that they may drink.  
<sup>4</sup>“Yes, and what are you to me, Tyre and Sidon,  
&emsp;&emsp;and all the regions of Philistia?  
Will you repay me?  
&emsp;&emsp;And if you repay me,  
&emsp;&emsp;I will swiftly and speedily return your repayment on your own head.  
<sup>5</sup>Because you have taken my silver and my gold,  
&emsp;&emsp;and have carried my finest treasures into your temples,  
&emsp;&emsp;<sup>6</sup>and have sold the children of Judah and the children of Jerusalem to the sons of the Greeks,  
&emsp;&emsp;that you may remove them far from their border.  
<sup>7</sup>Behold, I will stir them up out of the place where you have sold them,  
&emsp;&emsp;and will return your repayment on your own head;  
<sup>8</sup>and I will sell your sons and your daughters into the hands of the children of Judah,  
&emsp;&emsp;and they will sell them to the men of Sheba,  
&emsp;&emsp;to a faraway nation,  
&emsp;&emsp;for the LORD has spoken it.”  
<sup>9</sup>Proclaim this among the nations:  
&emsp;&emsp;“Prepare for war!  
&emsp;&emsp;Stir up the mighty men.  
Let all the warriors draw near.  
&emsp;&emsp;Let them come up. <sup>10</sup>Beat your plowshares into swords,  
&emsp;&emsp;and your pruning hooks into spears.  
&emsp;&emsp;Let the weak say, ‘I am strong.’  
<sup>11</sup>Hurry and come, all you surrounding nations,  
&emsp;&emsp;and gather yourselves together.”  
Cause your mighty ones to come down there, LORD.  
<sup>12</sup>“Let the nations arouse themselves,  
&emsp;&emsp;and come up to the valley of Jehoshaphat;  
&emsp;&emsp;for there I will sit to judge all the surrounding nations.  
<sup>13</sup>Put in the sickle;  
&emsp;&emsp;for the harvest is ripe.  
&emsp;&emsp;Come, tread, for the wine press is full,  
&emsp;&emsp;the vats overflow, for their wickedness is great.”  
<sup>14</sup>Multitudes, multitudes in the valley of decision!  
&emsp;&emsp;For the day of the LORD is near in the valley of decision.  
<sup>15</sup>The sun and the moon are darkened,  
&emsp;&emsp;and the stars withdraw their shining.  
<sup>16</sup>The LORD will roar from Zion,  
&emsp;&emsp;and thunder from Jerusalem;  
&emsp;&emsp;and the heavens and the earth will shake;  
&emsp;&emsp;but the LORD will be a refuge to his people,  
&emsp;&emsp;and a stronghold to the children of Israel.  
<sup>17</sup>“So you will know that I am the LORD, your God,  
&emsp;&emsp;dwelling in Zion, my holy mountain.  
Then Jerusalem will be holy,  
&emsp;&emsp;and no strangers will pass through her any more.  
<sup>18</sup>It will happen in that day,  
&emsp;&emsp;that the mountains will drop down sweet wine,  
&emsp;&emsp;the hills will flow with milk,  
&emsp;&emsp;all the brooks of Judah will flow with waters;  
&emsp;&emsp;and a fountain will flow out from the LORD’s house,  
&emsp;&emsp;and will water the valley of Shittim.  
<sup>19</sup>Egypt will be a desolation  
&emsp;&emsp;and Edom will be a desolate wilderness,  
&emsp;&emsp;for the violence done to the children of Judah,  
&emsp;&emsp;because they have shed innocent blood in their land.  
<sup>20</sup>But Judah will be inhabited forever,  
&emsp;&emsp;and Jerusalem from generation to generation.  
<sup>21</sup>I will cleanse their blood  
&emsp;&emsp;that I have not cleansed,  
&emsp;&emsp;for the LORD dwells in Zion.”
